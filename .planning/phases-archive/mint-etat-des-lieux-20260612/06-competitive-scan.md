# 06 — Competitive & Market Scan

## TLDR

- **« Qualité VZ »** concretely = an *independent, fee-based (Honorarberatung)* advisory model + multi-scenario written financial plans (lump-sum vs annuity, expenses/income/assets projected to and past retirement) + a free aggregation portal (VZ Finanzportal) that ranked **#1 in HSLU's 2025 digitalisation ranking for mobile/e-banking**. VZ is a CHF ~595M-revenue, ~1800-staff, SIX-listed group built over 30+ years. The moat is **trust + advisory liability absorption**, not software. Technology does not buy that.
- **The Swiss digital landscape is fragmented by product, not by life event.** finpension/VIAC/neon/Yuh own *3a execution*; True Wealth/Selma/Inyova own *robo investing*; Alpian owns *digital private banking + multi-currency expat*; UBS/Migros Bank/PostFinance own *bank-tied calculators*. **No one owns the cross-event, cross-institution lucidity layer** — that is MINT's white space. But it is white because it is *hard and unmonetised*, not because no one thought of it (Liiva tried and died).
- **Global AI-coach wave is real and monetising**: Cleo hit ~$280M ARR / ~1M paid subs by mid-2025 at 60% gross margin; Origin brands itself « industry's first AI financial advisor » with CFP humans behind it; Revolut shipped « AIR » to 13M UK users (Apr 2026). Retention = conversational engagement + advances/credit; trust posture = « AI supports, human/control retained ».
- **Trust gap is the central fact**: only **39%** of consumers (YouGov 2025) trust AI for financial *planning*; only **8%** comfortable with robo-advisors; 58% revert to humans after an AI money mistake. Accuracy + privacy + user-retains-control are the top 3 trust factors.
- **Regulatory**: CH is technology-neutral/principle-based — robo-advice inherits full fiduciary suitability/appropriateness duties (FINMA); LSFin no-promise rules already in MINT's DNA. EU AI Act classes creditworthiness-scoring advisors as **high-risk** (compliance deadline 2 Aug 2026) — relevant if MINT ever touches EU expats / credit scoring.
- **Wedge verdict (3 life-events × capability)**: (1) **Retirement/prévoyance decision-support** — lump-sum vs annuity *with confidence bands + editable assumptions*, beating VZ on accessibility and beating finpension/VIAC on scope; (2) **Cross-border / expat tax-prévoyance** (expat_eu, expat_us/FATCA, frontalier) — an archetype VZ serves expensively in-person and neobanks ignore; (3) **Housing affordability + amortisation/tax interplay** — the exact event Liiva failed to monetise, but MINT attacks it as *lucidity* not *brokerage*.
- **Founder challenge (mandatory)**: « toute la vie financière centralisée » as the *primary* wedge is a **graveyard** (Mint shut 2024 on aggregation-cost economics; Liiva CH shut 2024). Centralisation must be a *consequence* of solving a sharp decision, never the pitch. And the founder is **underestimating VZ's trust moat**: VZ's edge is that a human absorbs liability and the brand has 30 years of « they didn't sell me junk ». MINT cannot out-trust VZ with better UI; it can only out-*access* and out-*specificity* VZ for the segments VZ underserves.

## 1. VZ VermögensZentrum — Deep Dive

### What « VZ quality » concretely means

| Dimension | Concrete deliverable |
|---|---|
| **Advisory model** | Independent, fee-based **Honorarberatung** — financed only by client advisory fees, sells no proprietary products, passes all commissions to clients. This *independence claim* is the core trust asset. |
| **Written financial plan** | Shows how **expenses, income and assets develop until retirement and in the years after**. Presents **multiple scenarios** (e.g. pension capital as lump-sum vs annuity) to decide withdrawal split. This is the « scenario analysis with caveats » bar MINT must clear. |
| **Scope of advice** | Retirement, tax, investment, mortgages (Hypothekenzentrum AG), estate, insurance (VZ Versicherungszentrum AG), vested benefits (VZ Vorsorge AG), custody (VZ Depository Bank). One roof, 15 legal entities. |
| **Digital — VZ Finanzportal** | Free for clients, 24/7. Aggregates accounts, securities, pension funds, savings, real estate, mortgages, special assets + payments + trading + an **insurance directory with alerts** (« you can get better benefits / reduce premiums »). Property value calculator. **#1 in HSLU 2025 digitalisation ranking (mobile + e-banking).** |
| **VZ Finanzportal Pro** | Trading platform: real-time prices, recommendations on 200 Swiss stocks, analyses of 6500+ global stocks. |
| **Pricing** | Wealth management ~**0.7–1.4% p.a.** (DE figures; all-in flat-fee model, no hidden charges). Advisory billed hourly. Swiss depot: « Ticket Fee » or « Pauschalgebühr ». **Reputation: « Qualität hat ihren Preis » — professional but expensive.** Trustpilot ~3/5 (« acceptable »), mixed reviews: high competence praised, cost criticised. |
| **Scale / moat** | Founded 1993, SIX-listed since 2007, ~**1800 specialists**, CH+DE, ~**CHF 595M revenue (2025)**. |

### Strengths / weaknesses (for MINT to exploit)

- **Strengths**: independence narrative; 30-yr brand trust; human advisor absorbs liability; genuinely strong free portal; full-life-event scope under one roof.
- **Weaknesses MINT can attack**: **expensive** (high-net-worth-tilted, retiree-skewed); **human-bottlenecked** (you book an advisor, you don't self-serve a decision at 23:00); **retirement-first framing** (MINT's whole thesis is 18-99 life-event-equal); **aging digital UX despite ranking** — a portal is aggregation, not *coaching*; weak on **young / expat / frontalier / FATCA** segments who can't justify the fee or aren't VZ's ICP.

### White-space map

```
                       EXECUTION ----------------------> ADVICE/COACHING
  SINGLE PRODUCT   finpension/VIAC/neon/Yuh        bank pension hubs (conflicted)
  (3a, invest)     True Wealth/Selma/Inyova/Alpian  VZ advisor (human, costly)

  CROSS-EVENT      [ Liiva tried housing-cross — DIED ]   <-- MINT's target quadrant
  CROSS-INSTITUTION   *** NOBODY owns this digitally, free, life-event-segmented ***
```

**The white space is the bottom-right quadrant: cross-event + cross-institution + coaching, accessible (not CHF-1.4%-gated), 18-99.** It is empty because (a) aggregation economics are brutal (Mint, Liiva), and (b) advice triggers fiduciary liability. MINT's L1-chiffrer/offline + L2-L4 backend split and LSFin no-promise design are precisely an attempt to enter this quadrant *without* taking on robo-advice fiduciary status — coaching toward lucidity, not managing a mandate.

## 4. Research & Regulatory Signal

- **Trust gap (the headline number)**: YouGov 2025 — only **39%** of global respondents trust AI assistants with *financial planning* decisions; only **8%** comfortable with robo-advisors (US); **58%** would revert to a human after an AI money mistake. Top trust drivers: **privacy clarity (29%), accuracy (23%), user retains control (22%)**. ([rfi.global](https://rfi.global/bridging-the-ai-trust-gap-the-key-to-consumer-confidence-in-financial-services/), [FPA Journal Aug 2024](https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/learning/publications/journal/AUG24-customer-trust-and-satisfaction-robo-adviser-technology-OPEN), [ScienceDirect dual-trust](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969698925003169))
- **FINMA**: technology-neutral, principle-based. **Robo-advice = automated asset management → inherits full fiduciary suitability/appropriateness, best-interest, transparency-of-cost/risk, documentation duties.** Automation does **not** relieve accountability. FINMA eased *digital onboarding* (no written mandate required). ([Chambers Fintech 2025 CH](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/fintech-2025/switzerland/trends-and-developments), [FINMA Guidance](https://www.finma.ch/en/documentation/finma-guidance/))
- **EU AI Act**: robo-advisors scoring **creditworthiness/financial-capacity → high-risk**; compliance deadline **2 Aug 2026**; penalties up to €35M / 7% turnover; runs alongside DORA (live since 17 Jan 2025). Relevant the moment MINT serves EU expats or touches credit scoring. ([wealthAPI](https://wealthapi.eu/en/the-eu-ai-act-for-fintechs-and-banks-obligations-risks-and-compliance-steps/), [dialzara robo risks](https://dialzara.com/blog/ai-robo-advisors-regulatory-risks))
- **Implication for MINT**: MINT's existing posture (LSFin banned-terms, no-promise scenarios Bas/Moyen/Haut, confidence scores, no mandate management) is *exactly* the design that keeps it on the « coaching/lucidity » side of the fiduciary line. That is a moat-by-design — but it must never drift into implicit advice without the documentation infra.

## 5. Verdict — MINT's Defensible CH Wedge

**Three life-events × the capability that beats every incumbent:**

1. **Retirement / prévoyance decision-support** × **scenario lucidity with confidence bands + editable assumptions.**
   Beats VZ on *access* (free/cheap, self-serve, 24/7, no advisor booking) and beats finpension/VIAC/neon on *scope* (they execute 3a; they don't help you decide lump-sum vs annuity, vested-benefits timing, or 3a-vs-mortgage-amortisation trade-offs). The L4 invariant/confidence layer is the differentiator.

2. **Cross-border / expat tax-prévoyance** (expat_eu, **expat_us/FATCA**, frontalier) × **archetype-aware coaching.**
   VZ serves these expensively in person; neobanks (even Alpian's multi-currency) ignore the *planning* dimension; no one self-serves FATCA-aware or frontalier prévoyance logic. This is a defensible niche because it is *high-complexity, low-served, and MINT already models 8 archetypes* (NEVER #7). Hardest for incumbents to copy.

3. **Housing affordability + amortisation/tax interplay** × **lucidity, not brokerage.**
   This is the exact event Liiva attacked as a *marketplace/brokerage* and failed to monetise. MINT attacks it as a *decision* (« can I afford it, what does indirect amortisation do to my taxes and 3a »), which is a coaching surface, not a transaction-fee surface — so it dodges Liiva's economic trap.

**What « bien supérieur à VZ » requires that technology does NOT solve:**

- **Data accuracy / ground truth.** VZ's numbers come from human advisors reconciling real documents. MINT's superiority claim collapses the instant a projection is wrong. The `financial_core` canonical-layer discipline + Swiss-constants registry + forced-tool-invocation coaching (no « je n'ai pas cette donnée » when the value exists) is the *only* defence — and it is a perpetual-maintenance burden, not a one-time build.
- **Advisory liability absorption.** When VZ is wrong, a regulated human and a 30-yr balance sheet stand behind it. MINT, by design, does *not* take mandate liability — which is legally smart but means it must never *feel* like it's giving advice it can be sued over. The product must be superior at *lucidity* while being explicitly inferior at *taking responsibility*. That tension is permanent.
- **Brand trust at the moment of a 6-figure decision.** People will trust an app to tell them their net worth; the research says only 39% trust AI to *plan*. Trust is earned over years and one viral « MINT told me X and it cost me CHF 40k » erases it. Technology cannot shortcut this; only track record + radical caveating + human-escape-hatch can.

## 6. Challenge to the Founder's Framing (mandatory)

**Claim A — « toute la vie financière centralisée » is the winning wedge.**
**Pushback: this is a graveyard pitch, not a wedge.** The aggregator history is unambiguous: **Mint** (the category-defining aggregator) shut March 2024 because free aggregation loses money on every user and creates referral-conflict incentives. **Liiva** (Raiffeisen + Mobiliar, two of Switzerland's strongest balance sheets, explicit homeowner-life-event centralisation) shut July 2024 *despite promising PMF tests* — the « financial assistant » economics didn't sustain. Centralisation is a **feature that emerges after you've won a sharp decision**, never the headline. The winners (Cleo, Origin, Monarch) all lead with a *job-to-be-done* (coach me / advise me / forecast me), and aggregation is plumbing underneath. **Recommendation: MINT's pitch must be « lucidity on the decision in front of you » (housing, FATCA, lump-sum), and centralisation is the quiet by-product — never the marketing promise.** This also aligns with the existing pivot (« lucidité, pas protection ») and Rule 3 (life-event-equal, not centralisation-first).

**Claim B — implicit underestimation of VZ's trust moat.**
**Pushback: yes, he is underestimating it, and « on a toute la technologie » is the tell.** VZ's moat is *not* its software (MINT can plausibly beat the Finanzportal UX). VZ's moat is: (1) a *human absorbs the liability* of being wrong; (2) the *independence narrative* (« they sell no own products ») earned over 30 years; (3) the *trust to be handed a 6-figure retirement decision* — which the trust research says only ~39% of people will give to *any* AI. Technology does not transfer any of these three. **MINT cannot out-trust VZ on VZ's home turf (affluent near-retirees). It can only win the segments VZ underserves** (young, expat, frontalier, FATCA, the CHF-1.4%-fee-averse) by being *more accessible and more specific*, while being honest that it is *less liable*. « Bien supérieur à VZ » is achievable on **access + specificity + segment-fit**, and *not* achievable on **trust-at-the-big-decision** until MINT has its own track record. Conflating the two is the strategic risk.

**Net**: the technology bet is necessary but not sufficient. The defensible MINT is **a sharp life-event lucidity coach for VZ's underserved segments, monetised by subscription (Monarch model, never Cleo-advance/Mint-referral), riding the fiduciary-safe LSFin design as a feature** — with full-life centralisation as the retention flywheel that only switches on *after* the first sharp decision is won.

## Counter-arguments & data gaps (wiki-lint required)

**Counter-arguments to this scan's own conclusions:**
- *Maybe Liiva/Mint died for execution reasons, not thesis reasons.* Liiva was a brokerage/marketplace play (transaction fees) and Mint was ad/referral — MINT's subscription-lucidity model is a different animal, so the « graveyard » warning may be over-applied. Counter-counter: the *economics of free cross-institution data* still bite MINT identically; the warning holds on the cost side even if the monetisation differs.
- *VZ's trust moat may be eroding generationally* — under-40s may trust a good app more than a suited advisor, inverting the moat within MINT's 10-yr horizon. If true, the « can't out-trust VZ » caution weakens for the youngest cohort (which is also where MINT's wedge is).
- *Selma is closer to MINT than this scan credits* — its « financial helper » framing + life-aware questionnaire could pivot into coaching faster than expected. Worth a deeper standalone teardown.

**Data gaps (verify before betting strategy on this):**
- No firm VZ **AUM / client-count / branch-count** figure found (only revenue ~CHF 595M, ~1800 staff) — pull from VZ Holding annual report for sizing the moat precisely.
- Cleo/Origin/Monarch **retention/churn curves** are inferred from ARR + sub counts, not disclosed cohort retention — treat retention claims as directional.
- No primary **Swiss-specific** consumer-trust-in-AI-finance survey found; the 39%/8% figures are global (YouGov/US) — a CH-specific number could differ (Swiss trust-in-institutions is high, trust-in-novel-tech often lower).
- VZ Finanzportal's *actual* coaching depth (vs pure aggregation) not verified hands-on — the « it's aggregation not coaching » claim is inferred from feature descriptions, not a logged-in walkthrough.
- Whether MINT's L2-L4 backend outputs would legally read as « advice » under FINMA is **not** settled here — needs a compliance pass, not a competitive-scan assertion.
