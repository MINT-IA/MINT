### Findings

**P0** — none.

**P1** — none.

**P2**

1. **Facade-at-ledger-layer: no producer, no consumer, no DataQuest/dossier wiring yet.** `LppRegulationReference` is referenced only in the model and its own contract test — nothing writes it and nothing reads it (`Grep LppRegulationReference` → only `apps/mobile/lib/models/lpp_evidence.dart` and `test/models/lpp_regulation_reference_ledger_contract_test.dart`). Contrast the sibling `lppCapitalNoticeDeadline`, which is wired into `providers/coach_profile_provider.dart`, `screens/coach/retirement_dashboard_screen.dart`, and integration/patrol tests. This is acceptable for an incremental G1 ledger commit (it follows the RED `38edd8cda`), and it honestly adds no route/CTA it fails to deliver — hence P2, not P1. It becomes a P1 the moment a scenario claims "based on your fund's regulation" without the write-back path existing. Proof of the gap: no DataQuest question key or provider populates `LppEvidenceSnapshot.lppRegulationReference`.

2. **`source` semantically conflates the *règlement* with the *certificat*.** `lpp_evidence.dart:632,647` hardcode `'source': 'certificate'` for a regulation reference. In Swiss practice the *règlement de prévoyance* and the *certificat de prévoyance* are distinct documents. This is a wire enum denoting evidence grade (certificate-level) rather than the literal document type, and the strict decoder rejects `source: 'userInput'` (test `lpp_regulation_reference_ledger_contract_test.dart:163`), so it is coherent — but a distinct provenance token or a code comment would prevent future misreads.

3. **`legalYear` bound 1900–9999** (`lpp_evidence.dart:674-675`) is a sanity range, not a legal claim, so no current-law source is required. Noted only to confirm it was reviewed and is *not* a stale-constant risk.
